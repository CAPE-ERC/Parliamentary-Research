CAPE — Mauritius Parliamentary Discourse Research

Procedural Power & Policy Attention Pipeline

A four-layer computational pipeline over 411 Hansard debates of the Mauritius National Assembly (2015–2025), testing whether control over parliamentary procedure shapes which policy topics receive sustained attention. This page tracks implementation progress, methodology, and results layer by layer.

Every debate PDF is parsed with pdfplumber, segmented into speaker-attributed utterances via a regex tag pattern grounded in real Hansard formatting (including multi-line "long-form" tags like "The Vice-Prime Minister, Minister of Energy and Public Utilities (Mr I. Collendavelloo):"), and role/party-resolved using each debate's own printed Cabinet and Officers list — so ministerial reshuffles are picked up automatically per sitting rather than assumed static. Stage directions (interruptions, sitting suspensions) are kept as their own structural signal, not folded into speech.

fastText → langdetect substitution. The milestone spec calls for fastText language ID; it requires a C++ build toolchain unavailable on this machine. langdetect plus a Kreol Morisien keyword heuristic was used instead — documented in code, not silently swapped.
Role-resolution refinement in progress. A gap was found where generic role titles — "The Deputy Speaker," "The Deputy Prime Minister," "The Chairperson" — weren't matched by the original role patterns and fell into unclassified_mp. Fixed in src/preprocessing/roles.py; the corpus is being re-processed now.

Linking Layer

In progress Intervention rate ~ topic × speaker party × chair

Why this is taking real work, not a quick regression. Grounding the model against real data found that only 145 of 211,567 utterances had a resolved party = opposition — and every one came from a single office (Leader of Opposition) — versus 39,396 government. 65,610 backbench MP utterances (both sides) had no party at all. Running H1 (Government vs. Opposition asymmetry) on that data would really be testing "Leader of Opposition vs. Ministers," not a party-level effect.

Done

  • Researched & verified real Mauritius National Assembly membership for all 3 elections spanning the corpus (2014 / 2019 / 2024 → 6th / 7th / 8th Assembly) — ~198 MP records with party and constituency
  • Verified and encoded a real mid-term coalition change: PMSD left the governing Alliance Lepep on 19 Dec 2016 — government/opposition status is date-aware, not a static per-term label
  • Found and fixed an upstream Layer 0 gap: generic chair/government titles ("The Deputy Speaker," "The Chairperson," "The Deputy Prime Minister") weren't being role-resolved

Next

  • Re-run Layer 0 with the role-resolution fix
  • Resolve backbench-MP party from the registry, scoped by Assembly term and sitting date
  • Extract per-debate presiding-chair identity
  • Build the topic × party × chair panel; fit the mixed-effects model (H1) and a secondary PNQ-deflection analysis (H2)

Not started Topic attention share in t+1 ~ procedural conflict in t
